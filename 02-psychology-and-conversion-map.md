# 02 — Psychology and conversion map

The aim is to understand people deeply and serve them well, never to trick them. Every principle below has an **ethical limit**. If a use would break it, don't build it.

## 1. How people will arrive

- **Mostly on phones**, often from a WhatsApp message, a QR code on a poster, or a LinkedIn post.
- **With a question in mind:** "Is this real? Is it for me? What do I get? What does it take?"
- **Judging fast.** Classic research (Lindgaard and colleagues, 2006) found that people form a visual-appeal judgement of a web page in about 50 milliseconds. Nielsen Norman Group's research found that content above the fold receives far more attention than content below it (an average difference of 84%).

**So:** the first screen must answer "what is this and is it for me" instantly, look exceptional, and offer one clear next step.

## 2. The principles, where they live, and their limits

| Principle | What it says | Where we use it | Ethical limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aesthetic-usability effect** | People perceive beautiful designs as easier to use and more trustworthy | The whole noble visual system | Beauty never hides information or makes forms harder |
| **Self-reference and identity** | People pay attention to what is about them and who they are | Two-shores scene; audience pages open with "You..." | Represent every audience honestly, including women and international graduates |
| **Customer as hero (the StoryBrand framework)** | People follow a guide who gives them a plan | Every audience page: outcome → problem → plan → change → invitation | The plan must be the real process |
| **Hick's law** | More choices mean slower decisions | One primary CTA per view; four doors only | Secondary options stay visible, never hidden |
| **Isolation (Von Restorff) effect** | The one item that differs is remembered | The single palm-green primary button per view | — |
| **Serial position effect** | People remember the first and last items best | Key benefits placed first and last in lists; the promise closes the page | — |
| **Cognitive fluency** | Easy-to-read means easy-to-believe | Short sentences, high contrast, one idea per block | Never simplify away important conditions |
| **Commitment and consistency (Cialdini)** | A small yes leads to a bigger yes | Forms start with the easiest question ("I am a..."); "About 2 minutes" | Time estimates must be accurate |
| **Endowed progress (Nunes and Drèze, 2006)** | People are more likely to finish when progress already appears started | The form progress thread shows "Step 1 of 4 — you've started" once the first choice is made | Progress shown must be real |
| **Goal-gradient effect (Kivetz and colleagues, 2006)** | Effort increases as the goal gets closer | The journey thread; the final form step says "Last step" | — |
| **Unfinished-task pull (Zeigarnik effect)** | Unfinished tasks stay on the mind | The confirmation screen shows the journey with "Join ✓" and the next step highlighted | No nagging emails; one reminder at most, opt-in |
| **Ownership (IKEA effect; Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012)** | People value what they helped build | "Founding members help shape the society"; a question on how they'd like to contribute | Only promise the influence we will genuinely give |
| **Reciprocity (Cialdini)** | People want to return a favour | Free places, free workshops, free downloads with no email gate | Free really means free |
| **Unity and belonging (Cialdini)** | "People like us" is a strong motivator | "Our community", founding member language, dual-script identity | Never exclude or rank people by nationality |
| **Scarcity (Cialdini)** | Rare things feel more valuable | "One seat per industry"; "Founding places" | Only true limits. Live counts only from real data behind a flag. No timers |
| **Social proof (Cialdini)** | People follow others like them | Testimonial, partner logo, speaker and counter components | **Hidden until real.** No stock faces, no invented quotes |
| **Authority (Cialdini)** | Credible experts persuade | Speaker and mentor cards, advisory council section | Real people only, with their permission |
| **Liking (Cialdini)** | People say yes to those they like | Warm voice, founder note, faces of real members later | Honest founder story only |
| **Loss framing (used lightly)** | Losses feel bigger than equal gains | "Don't let your degree wait on a network" (one use, graduates page) | Never fear-based or shaming |
| **Choice architecture and defaults** | Defaults steer decisions | Sensible defaults (language from browser, "prefer to discuss" for budget) | Consent is never a default |
| **Peak-end rule (Kahneman)** | Experiences are judged by their peak and end | The invitation-card confirmation; the dusk closing scene | — |
| **Self-determination theory** | People thrive with autonomy, competence and relatedness | Four doors (autonomy), the Learn stage (competence), circles (relatedness) | — |
| **Costly signalling in luxury** | Care and restraint signal quality | Craft details, slowness, space | Luxury feel must not imply high fees or exclusion |

## 3. Audience-by-audience plan

### Graduates
- **Core desire:** "I want a real start: a job, clients, or my own business, and people who believe in me."
- **Core fear:** "I'll send applications into silence." For international graduates: "Will I belong here?"
- **Message order:**
  1. Outcome
  2. The silence problem
  3. The six-step journey
  4. Six benefits
  5. Who can join, plus free places and women's circles
  6. Honest FAQ (founding stage, what membership costs, where events happen)
  7. "Apply to become a founding member"
- **Primary CTA:** Apply to become a founding member.
- **Micro-yes:** "Join the WhatsApp community first."

### Universities
- **Core desire:** better graduate outcomes and industry links with no cost or risk.
- **Core fear:** reputational risk from an unknown group.
- **Message order:**
  1. Outcome
  2. What we bring
  3. What we ask (small and clear)
  4. What the university gains
  5. Four-step partnership with reporting
  6. Governance and founding-stage honesty
  7. "Host a campus chapter"
- **Micro-yes:** "Download the one-pager."

### Sponsors
- **Core desire:** measurable impact, visibility and first access to talent, aligned with national goals for youth and women's employment.
- **Core fear:** money disappearing without results.
- **Message order:**
  1. Outcome
  2. Why sponsor
  3. Six options
  4. What every sponsor receives
  5. Sample impact report layout, labelled illustrative
  6. "Become a founding sponsor"
- **Micro-yes:** "Download the prospectus."

### Businesses
- **Core desire:** clients, partners and good hires, and a respected voice.
- **Core fear:** another networking event that wastes time.
- **Message order:**
  1. Outcome
  2. Three ways (networking, keynote, hiring)
  3. "Bring us a real challenge"
  4. One seat per industry
  5. Getting started in three steps
  6. "Take your seat at the table"
- **Micro-yes:** "Attend a Business Circle as a guest."

## 4. Microcopy standards

- **Button labels** say what happens next ("Apply for membership", "Book a 20-minute call"), never "Submit".
- **Error messages** are kind and specific: "Please add your graduation year so we can match you to the right circle." / "يرجى إضافة سنة التخرج لنرشّح لك الحلقة المناسبة."
- **Reassurance** sits under each CTA: "Free to apply. No obligation. We reply within `RESPONSE_TIME`."
- **Empty states** feel like anticipation, not absence: "First events announced soon. Be the first to know."

## 5. What we measure

- **Funnel:** door click → form start → each step → submit → WhatsApp join.
- **By source:** every `/go/[campaign]` route and `utm_*` parameter.
- **North star:** Opportunities created (jobs, contracts, referrals, startups), entered by the owner in `/admin`.
- **Guardrails:** form abandonment by step, 404s, Core Web Vitals, accessibility errors.
- **A/B tests:** headlines and CTA labels only, one at a time, with enough traffic before judging. Never test anything deceptive.
