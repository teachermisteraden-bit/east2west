## 2. Patterns from luxury houses (general principles)

The best luxury fashion, jewellery and watch sites consistently share these traits:
- **Space and restraint:** big pauses, few words per screen.
- **Typography as the hero:** a high-contrast serif, used large.
- **Slow motion** that feels like material (fabric, light, stone).
- **Deliberate calls to action:** "Discover", "Request an appointment", "Enquire", framed as invitations.
- **Craft in micro-interactions:** hover lines, cursor, transitions.
- **Storytelling** about heritage, makers and values before products.
- **Service language:** "our advisers", "by appointment", "your enquiry".

Our adaptation: invitation language ("Apply for membership", "Take your seat", "Host a chapter") on top of truly inclusive substance.

## 3. Usability research we follow

- **Don't hijack scrolling.** A Nielsen Norman Group usability study found that most participants were at least mildly disoriented by altered scroll behaviour, some thought it was a bug, task-focused users tolerated it least, and it was worse on mobile.
  - Our rule: native scroll, at most two short sticky scenes, desktop only.
  - Sources: https://dontfuckwithscroll.com/ (summary of the research) and https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-scrollbars/
- **The first screen matters most.** Nielsen Norman Group reports that people treat content above the fold very differently from content below it (an average difference of 84%), and scroll only when they have a reason to.
  - Our rule: the hero states the offer and gives one action, and a scroll cue gives a reason to continue.
  - Source: https://www.nngroup.com/videos/scrolling-information-foraging/
- **People form a visual judgement in about 50 ms** (Lindgaard et al., 2006, *Behaviour & Information Technology*). Our rule: the first paint must already look finished (no loaders, no layout shift).
